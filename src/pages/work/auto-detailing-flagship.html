01The brief

A considered purchase deserves a site that shows it.

Paint protection film, ceramic coating and window tint are researched purchases. Before anyone books, they want to know which panels a package covers, how dark a shade really is, and when the shop can take the car.

Most detailing sites answer with a gallery and a phone number. This one answers each question with something to try: turn the car, switch the coverage, slide the tint, then send a booking request that already says what the car needs.

It was built first for AZ Detailers, a paint protection and ceramic studio in Phoenix. The same build is ready for any studio that sells high-ticket protection work.

03Seven pages

Seven pages, one file of facts.

Everything on the site comes from one brief about the business, with a source noted for every fact: its own website, its Google listing and what the owner says. Change the brief and the whole site rebuilds in seconds.

  1. 01

    Home

    The name over a 3D car the visitor can turn and repaint, the services with their prices, a before and after, Google reviews, hours with a live "Open now" sign, a map, and buttons to call, text and book.

  2. 02

    Services

    Each service in depth: what's included, the packages and their prices, the coverage switcher, a ceramic durability scale and the tint previewer.

  3. 03

    Work

    The shop's own photographs, filtered by service, opening full screen with the keyboard or a swipe.

  4. 04

    Book

    Vehicle, services, then the customer's details and an open day. The request reaches the shop by email, and the shop confirms.

  5. 05

    Privacy

    Plain English, written from what the site actually does with a booking request.

  6. 06

    Terms

    Plain English as well. Either legal page can be switched off with one setting.

  7. 07

    Not found

    A page for wrong addresses, in the same design, with the way back to the rest of the site.

04How it's built

A compiler that refuses to guess

Client brief
One file of facts about the business, each with its source. A brief with anything left to fill in doesn't build.
Compiler
Python. It merges the brief over the studio's catalogue of services, renders seven HTML templates and checks its own output: nothing left unfilled, every link and image resolving, one main heading a page.
Images
Turned upright, location data stripped, saved as WebP at 640, 1280 and 1920 pixels, and cached, so a rebuild takes about two seconds.
3D showroom
three.js, the only bundled script. It loads where the device can run it. With reduced motion, without WebGL or with data saving on, the visitor gets a still image of the same car.
Structured data
The business, its FAQ, its prices and its breadcrumbs as JSON-LD, written from the same brief.
Booking
A form service emails each request to the shop. The site never takes payment and never confirms a booking on its own.

What it won't do

Show a price the owner hasn't confirmed, label a testimonial from the shop's own site as a Google review, or show opening hours nobody gave it.
