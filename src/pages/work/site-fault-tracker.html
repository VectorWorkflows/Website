01The problem

Field reports, scattered across chats.

When a field team spots a site fault, a damaged installation or something that needs logging, the report ends up wherever it's easiest to send: a text, a WhatsApp photo, a voice note in a group chat.

Nothing gets structured, nobody can find it later, and chasing people to fill in forms becomes its own job. The value here is simple: every report lands as one clean, structured record, sent from the app the team already has open.

03How it works

Photo in, structured record out.

  1. 01

    A worker sends a photo and a note

    From WhatsApp, the app field staff already use. There's no new software to install and no form to fill in.

  2. 02

    The bot acknowledges it

    A quick reply confirms the report arrived, so nobody sends it twice or wonders whether it landed.

  3. 03

    The team's own words are kept

    The photo and the worker's description are logged as sent. Extra details are pulled out where the message has them, like the location it mentions.

  4. 04

    A rough severity flag is added

    A vision model looks at the photo and suggests a severity. It's a first-pass indicator for sorting, never a precise rating.

  5. 05

    One timestamped row, ready for review

    Everything lands as a single row in the team's Google Sheet, where someone can review it, act on it and find it again later.

04Stack

What it runs on

WhatsApp Cloud API
Receives the photo and note, sends the acknowledgement and summary
Python
The bot service: parsing the message and assembling the record
Vision model
A first-pass look at the photo for the rough severity flag
Google Sheets
The team's log: one row per report, with the photo attached

What it doesn't do

The severity flag is a rough first-pass estimate. Photo quality and site conditions vary too much for it to be a precise rating, which is why every row is there for a person to review.
