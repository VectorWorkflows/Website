Hand-built static websites vs WordPress and Wix: speed, cost and bookings

For a small service business, a website has three jobs: open fast on a phone, cost what you expected for years, and turn a visitor into a call, a message or a booking. Here's how the three ways of building one compare on each, including where a builder wins.

What "hand-built" means

A hand-built static site is a set of plain files, HTML, CSS and a little JavaScript, written for one business and served as they are. There's no content management system behind it, no database, no page builder and no admin login. When a visitor opens a page, the server hands over the finished page; nothing is assembled on the fly.

WordPress builds each page from a database, a theme and plugins. Wix and Squarespace are builders: you design on their platform, with their editor, and they host the result.

Speed, measured

The page you're reading weighs of HTML, CSS and JavaScript, compressed, calculator and replay included. With the fonts that load straight away, our home page comes to 189 kB in total and the services page to 193 kB, measured with Lighthouse on 23 October 2026.

For comparison, the median mobile web page in 2024 weighed 2,311 kB, according to the HTTP Archive's Web Almanac. Weight isn't everything, but on a mid-range phone over mobile data every one of those kilobytes is something to download and run before the page is useful.

Google's yardstick for a page that feels fast is its Core Web Vitals: the main content should appear within 2.5 seconds, the page should respond to a tap within 200 milliseconds, and it shouldn't jump around while loading (web.dev). Google recommends meeting them "for success with Search". Any platform can meet them; a hand-built site starts with far less to strip away.

Cost over the years

A builder or managed WordPress plan is a monthly cost for as long as the site is up, and paid themes, plugins and booking apps add their own. A hand-built site is paid for once. With us, year one includes hosting, your domain, SSL and small changes; after that it keeps running, you renew the domain at the market price, and a care plan is optional.

Upkeep and security

A WordPress site is software that needs looking after: the core, the theme and each plugin get updates, and each plugin is someone else's code running on your site. Builders handle that for you, which is a real advantage. A static site has very little to look after: with no database and no login page, the usual ways in aren't there.

  • WordPress: update the core, the theme and every plugin, and keep backups.
  • Wix or Squarespace: the platform updates itself; you live within what its editor and apps allow.
  • Hand-built: nothing to update on your side; changes are made to the files and published again.

Bookings

A site earns its keep when it turns a visitor into a call, a message or a booking. The things that matter most are not platform features: a page that opens quickly, a way to act on every screen (call, WhatsApp, book), and a request that still works at 9 pm. When a builder is the better choice

  • You want to change pages yourself every week, add posts, rearrange sections. A builder's editor is made for that.
  • You're running a real online shop, with stock, shipping and payments. That isn't what we build.
  • You need something today and can't spend anything yet. A free builder plan, with the builder's branding, beats having no site.

For a service business whose site's job is to be found, open fast and bring in calls, messages and bookings, hand-built is the better trade. That's the only kind of site we make, and the detailing flagship and Reliance Coaching Centre are two of them.

Not for being hand-built. Google ranks pages that answer the search well. It does recommend good Core Web Vitals, and a light, fast site makes those easier to meet.
