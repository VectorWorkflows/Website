Case Study — 01

Dynamic
Scheduler

The Problem

Rigid Schedules Fail Real Life

Most scheduling tools assume your day will go exactly as planned. When something runs over, gets cancelled, or needs rearranging, a static calendar stays where it is — leaving you with a plan that no longer fits your actual day. The cycle: build a schedule, fall behind, abandon it, repeat.

What We Built

A Bot That Adapts Instead of Breaking

A Telegram bot that creates and dynamically reschedules Google Calendar events on request. When a task needs to move, you send the bot the clear and add command and it rearranges your calendar to match — adapting to reality instead of pretending it didn't change. No new app to learn; it lives inside Telegram.

How It Works

Plain Language. Real Calendar Changes.

Send a message — "Clear Day", "Add Task" — and the bot parses the intent, finds the relevant event, and deletes or adds it in Google Calendar. It can create new events, reschedule existing ones, and keep your day coherent as things shift. The walkthrough video shows the full flow: a Miro board framing the rigid vs. dynamic scheduling problem, followed by a live calendar demo.

Build Log — 02

Site Fault
Tracker

The Problem

Field Reports Scattered Across Chats

When field teams spot a site fault — a damaged installation, something that needs logging — the report ends up wherever it's easiest to send: a text, a WhatsApp photo, a voice note in a group chat. Nothing gets structured, nobody can find it later, and chasing people to fill in forms becomes its own job.

What We Built

Photo In, Structured Record Out

A WhatsApp bot where field staff photograph a site fault and send it directly from the app already on their phones. The bot passes each image through an AI vision model, extracts the relevant details, and logs a structured row into a shared Google Sheet — no manual data entry, no missed fields, no chasing anyone to fill in a form.

How It Works

Send a Photo. Get a Logged Record.

A field worker photographs the fault and sends it to the WhatsApp number. The bot receives the image, runs it through a vision model to identify and describe what's wrong, then appends a timestamped structured row to a Google Sheet — fault description, severity, and photo reference all captured automatically, with no human in the loop.

Status
Fully Built — Deployment-Ready

This is a self-initiated project — built to prove the pattern before bringing it to a client. The bot is complete and tested end-to-end. Like any WhatsApp Business integration, it needs to be connected to a dedicated business number to go live — standard setup for any client deployment, done at onboarding rather than in advance for a demo. Build Log — 03

Missed Lead
Responder

The Problem

A Missed Call Is a Lost Customer

For most local and service businesses, an unanswered phone is one of the most expensive gaps in the whole operation. A caller who doesn't leave a voicemail is a caller who just tried the next business on the list instead. By the time anyone notices the missed call sitting in the log, the window to win that customer back has usually already closed.

What We Built

Instant Follow-Up, Zero Manual Chasing

A responder that watches for missed calls the moment they happen and fires back an automatic follow-up within seconds — acknowledging the caller, answering the obvious first question, and logging the contact as a lead — so nothing falls through the gap between "the phone rang" and "someone followed up."

How It Works

Missed Call In. Structured Lead Out.

The moment a call goes unanswered, the responder triggers an automatic reply to the caller and writes a structured lead record — number, timestamp, and status — so the business always has a live, current list of who called and whether they've been brought back into the conversation.
